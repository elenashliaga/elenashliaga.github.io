Every week I will publish one article about physics here. This post explains what to expect, what not to expect, and why I am doing this.

## Why I am writing

I am a physicist working on edge-emitting semiconductor lasers — lasing thresholds, spectra, mode competition, and the numerics of Maxwell's equations that describe them ([about me](/about/)). Like most researchers, I read many papers and explain much of what I learn to students and colleagues. Writing forces that explanation to be complete. A blog makes it public, searchable, and — I hope — useful to someone who would otherwise spend an evening stuck on the same idea.

There is also a selfish reason: I want to be part of the international physics conversation. The best way to be found is to write clearly about things people are searching for.

## What you will find here

- **Quantum computing, explained carefully.** Qubits, superposition, gates, decoherence, error correction — built from first principles, with formulas only when they help. The goal is that a physics student can actually follow every step.
- **Photonics and lasers.** How a laser reaches threshold, what gain and modes really are, how gratings and mirrors select a single wavelength. This is my home territory, and I will keep it textbook-honest.
- **Paper digests.** Short, careful summaries of research papers — what problem they solve, how, and what the limits are. Every digest links the original paper, and I only describe what the authors actually claim.

Posts appear weekly, in English. Math is rendered properly, so when a formula is the clearest way to say something, it will be there — always with a reference to where it comes from.

## What you will NOT find here

My own unpublished research results do not appear on this blog — they go to peer-reviewed journals first. Everything published here is already-published knowledge, re-explained. When I digest my own published papers, I will say so explicitly.

## One more thing

If a post helps you, or if you think I got something wrong, write to me: [elena.shliaga@gmail.com](mailto:elena.shliaga@gmail.com). Corrections make the blog better, and disagreeing is what physics is built on.

See you next week.
